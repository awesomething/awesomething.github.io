# AI Literacy Curriculum for Kindergarten (Age 5)
## Inspired by Maryland's AI Ready Schools Act (SB 720, effective June 1, 2026)

*A structured, fun, and engaging 36-week program designed for a 5-year-old boy — with a weekly "Vibe Coding Friday" elective.*

---

## Background

Maryland became one of the first states to require AI literacy across K–12 education. The Artificial Intelligence Ready Schools Act (SB 720), sponsored by Senators Brooks, Hester, Harris, Attar, Feldman, and Watson, went into effect on June 1, 2026, requiring every Maryland school system to incorporate AI literacy into workforce preparation and computer science standards from kindergarten through grade 12 by June 2027.

As Senator Hester noted, every student will graduate into a world where nearly every career uses AI in some way, and it's our responsibility to make sure they're prepared.

This curriculum translates that mandate into a parent-friendly, play-based program for a 5-year-old.

**Source:** [AfroTech — This State Just Passed A Bill Requiring AI Literacy For K-12 Students](https://afrotech.com/this-state-just-passed-a-bill-requiring-ai-literacy-for-k-12-students)

## Core Principles (What a 5-Year-Old CAN Learn)

| ✅ Teach This | ❌ NOT This (Save for Age 8+) |
|---|---|
| AI is made by people | Neural networks |
| AI follows patterns and rules | Algorithms (abstract) |
| AI can be a helpful tool | Data privacy concepts |
| AI makes mistakes | Coding syntax |
| Humans are unique — we feel, create, care | Machine learning math |

**Session Rules:**
- Keep every activity to **15–20 minutes** max
- Always frame AI as **a tool, not magic**: "The computer learned from examples people showed it"
- **Celebrate errors**: When AI gets something wrong, that's the best learning moment
- **Make it physical**: The best AI lessons at this age use bodies, objects, and movement
- No testing or grading — **exposure and wonder** are the goals

---

## Weekly Schedule Overview

| Day | Focus | Duration |
|---|---|---|
| **Monday** | AI Concept of the Week (unplugged activity) | 15–20 min |
| **Tuesday** | Storytime + Discussion (AI-themed book or story) | 15 min |
| **Wednesday** | Hands-On AI Tool Exploration (screen time) | 15–20 min |
| **Thursday** | Creative Project / Craft (reinforce the week's concept) | 20 min |
| **Friday** | 🎮 **Vibe Coding Elective** (see Section below) | 20–30 min |

## Semester 1: "What Is AI?" (Weeks 1–18)

### Unit 1: AI Is All Around Us (Weeks 1–4)

**Week 1 — "Smart Helpers at Home"**
- **Mon (Unplugged):** Walk around the house — point out devices that "seem smart" (Alexa, phone autocomplete, YouTube suggestions). Ask: "Is this alive? Does it think?"
- **Tue (Story):** Tell a story about a robot that can sort toys by color but doesn't know what "favorite" means
- **Wed (Tool):** [Google Quick, Draw!](https://quickdraw.withgoogle.com/) — draw things and watch AI try to guess. Ask: "Did it know what it was? Or just match a pattern?"
- **Thu (Craft):** Draw "My Robot Friend" — what can it do? What can't it do?
- **Fri (Vibe Coding):** ScratchJr — Introduction: open the app, meet the cat, tap the green flag

**Week 2 — "People Make AI"**
- **Mon:** Role play — one person is the "robot," the other gives instructions to make a sandwich. The robot only does EXACTLY what you say (literal instructions = how computers work)
- **Tue:** Read *Hello Ruby* by Linda Liukas (or watch the read-aloud on YouTube)
- **Wed:** [Google AutoDraw](https://www.autodraw.com/) — doodle a cat, watch AI suggest clean versions. "YOU decided what to draw. AI is the helper, not the boss."
- **Thu:** Make a "How I Taught My Robot" comic strip (3 panels)
- **Fri (Vibe Coding):** ScratchJr — make the cat walk across the screen

**Week 3 — "AI Follows Rules"**
- **Mon:** Pattern game with colored blocks: Red-Blue-Red-Blue-___? "You just did what AI does! AI finds patterns and predicts what comes next."
- **Tue:** Story about a pattern-finding robot that gets tricked by a new color
- **Wed:** Quick, Draw! again — this time, draw something silly on purpose and watch AI get confused
- **Thu:** Make a "Pattern Necklace" with beads — create a pattern, then predict the next bead
- **Fri (Vibe Coding):** ScratchJr — add a second character and make them talk

**Week 4 — "AI Makes Mistakes"**
- **Mon:** Play "Guess What I'm Thinking" — give clues about an animal. Sometimes the guess is wrong! "Even AI gets wrong answers."
- **Tue:** Story about a spell-checker that changes words to funny wrong words
- **Wed:** Use AutoDraw — draw something abstract and see how hilariously wrong the suggestions can be. Celebrate the mistakes!
- **Thu:** "Oops! AI Art" — intentionally make bad drawings and laugh at AI's guesses. Collage the funniest results
- **Fri (Vibe Coding):** ScratchJr — record your own voice for a character

### Unit 2: How Does AI Learn? (Weeks 5–9)

**Week 5 — "Sorting Like AI"**
- **Mon:** Sorting game — sort toy animals into groups (by color, size, legs). Then ask: "What if we sort by something different? Is a tomato a fruit or a vegetable?"
- **Tue:** Story about a robot that sorts mail but puts birthday cards in the "boring letters" pile
- **Wed:** [Google Teachable Machine](https://teachablemachine.withgoogle.com/) (parent-guided) — train the computer to recognize hand gestures
- **Thu:** Make a "Sorting Machine" out of cardboard boxes — label categories, sort household items
- **Fri (Vibe Coding):** ScratchJr — make a simple animation (character + background)

**Week 6 — "Learning From Examples"**
- **Mon:** Show 10 photos of cats and 10 photos of dogs. "If I showed the computer thousands of these, it could learn to tell them apart — just like you!"
- **Tue:** Story about training a pet vs. training a computer
- **Wed:** Quick, Draw! — notice how AI gets better at recognizing common objects vs. rare ones
- **Thu:** "Train Your Friend" game — show a friend 5 pictures of your favorite animal, then test them with a tricky one
- **Fri (Vibe Coding):** ScratchJr — make a character react when you tap it

**Week 7 — "Patterns in Music"**
- **Mon:** Clap a rhythm pattern. Can you predict what comes next? "AI listens for patterns in music too!"
- **Tue:** Listen to a song, then a computer-generated melody. "Which one makes you feel something?"
- **Wed:** [Chrome Music Lab](https://musiclab.chromeexperiments.com/) — explore Song Maker and Rhythm
- **Thu:** Make a "Pattern Song" with pots, pans, and spoons
- **Fri (Vibe Coding):** ScratchJr — add sounds to your animation

**Week 8 — "AI Sees Pictures"**
- **Mon:** Play "I Spy" but describe things the way a computer would: "I see something round and red" (not "I see something yummy")
- **Tue:** Story about a robot that thinks a fire hydrant is a short red person
- **Wed:** [Google Lens](https://lens.google/) (on parent's phone) — point it at objects and see what AI thinks they are
- **Thu:** Draw an object from 3 angles. "AI needs lots of views to understand things too!"
- **Fri (Vibe Coding):** ScratchJr — build a 2-page story

**Week 9 — "AI and Words"**
- **Mon:** Play "Finish My Sentence" — start a sentence, let your child complete it. "That's what phones do when they suggest the next word!"
- **Tue:** Silly story where autocorrect changes every word wrong
- **Wed:** Parent types something into phone keyboard — show predictive text suggestions. "The phone guesses from patterns of what people type."
- **Thu:** Write a 3-sentence story together, then change one word to something silly. "AI doesn't always pick the right word either!"
- **Fri (Vibe Coding):** ScratchJr — free build day (make anything you want!)

### Unit 3: AI and Me (Weeks 10–14)

**Week 10 — "AI Is a Tool, Like a Hammer"**
- **Mon:** Line up tools: hammer, paintbrush, calculator, tablet. "Which ones are alive? Which ones think? None! They're all tools that help people."
- **Tue:** Story about a boy who uses different tools for different jobs
- **Wed:** AutoDraw — create a "My Toolbox" poster using AI-assisted drawings
- **Thu:** Build something with LEGO. "YOU are the designer. The blocks are the tool."
- **Fri (Vibe Coding):** ScratchJr — make a character navigate a maze

**Week 11 — "Things Only Humans Can Do"**
- **Mon:** Discuss: Can AI give a hug? Can AI be your best friend? Can AI imagine a brand new animal?
- **Tue:** Story about things robots wish they could do (feel rain, taste ice cream, dream)
- **Wed:** Use Quick, Draw! — after the game, try drawing something that has never existed. "AI can only copy patterns from things people already made. YOU can imagine new things."
- **Thu:** Invent and draw a brand-new animal. Name it. Where does it live?
- **Fri (Vibe Coding):** ScratchJr — animate your invented animal

**Week 12 — "Staying Safe"**
- **Mon:** Practice: "What's your name?" "I don't share that with computers." Role-play safe vs. unsafe sharing
- **Tue:** Story about a friendly robot that asks too many questions
- **Wed:** Parent-guided: explore a kid-safe voice assistant. Practice saying "I need to ask a grown-up"
- **Thu:** Make a "Safe Online" poster with rules
- **Fri (Vibe Coding):** ScratchJr — add a "safety message" to a story project

**Week 13 — "Asking Good Questions"**
- **Mon:** "20 Questions" game — practice asking yes/no questions to narrow things down. "This is how search engines find answers!"
- **Tue:** Story about a kid who asks the right questions to solve a mystery
- **Wed:** Parent types a question into a search engine together. See how different questions give different results
- **Thu:** Draw 3 questions you'd like to ask a robot
- **Fri (Vibe Coding):** ScratchJr — build a quiz game (character asks a question, you pick an answer)

**Week 14 — Semester 1 Showcase**
- **Mon–Thu:** Review favorite activities. Pick your best ScratchJr project and best craft
- **Fri:** 🎉 **Show & Tell** — present your favorite project to family. Explain one thing about AI

## Semester 2: "AI and the World" (Weeks 19–36)

### Unit 4: AI Helpers in the Real World (Weeks 19–23)

**Week 19 — "AI in the Doctor's Office"**
- **Mon:** Discuss: How does a doctor know you're sick? What if a computer could help by looking at X-rays?
- **Wed:** Look at simple X-ray images online together — play "find the broken bone"
- **Fri (Vibe Coding):** ScratchJr — make a "Doctor Robot" character that asks how you feel

**Week 20 — "AI and Animals"**
- **Mon:** Discuss: Scientists use cameras and AI to count animals in the wild so they can protect them
- **Wed:** Watch a short wildlife camera clip — try to count animals yourself. "Now imagine a computer doing that ALL day!"
- **Fri (Vibe Coding):** ScratchJr — build an animal-counting game

**Week 21 — "AI and Weather"**
- **Mon:** Look at a 5-day weather forecast. "The computer looks at patterns from MILLIONS of past weather days to guess tomorrow."
- **Wed:** Make your own "weather predictor" — look outside, check the sky, make a prediction. Compare with the app!
- **Fri (Vibe Coding):** ScratchJr — make a weather scene that changes

**Week 22 — "AI and Cars"**
- **Mon:** Discuss self-driving cars. Use toy cars — "What rules would you give a robot car?"
- **Wed:** Build a road from tape on the floor. Be the "robot car" — someone else gives directions. What happens when instructions are bad?
- **Fri (Vibe Coding):** ScratchJr — program a car to drive along a path

**Week 23 — "AI and Art"**
- **Mon:** Compare a hand-drawn picture to an AutoDraw version. "Which one has more feeling?"
- **Wed:** Create a side-by-side: your drawing vs. AI's version of the same thing
- **Fri (Vibe Coding):** ScratchJr — make an interactive art gallery

### Unit 5: Being a Good AI Citizen (Weeks 24–28)

**Week 24 — "Fair or Unfair?"**
- **Mon:** Sort a pile of mixed items using ONLY one rule (e.g., only by color). "Did we miss something important? When AI sorts by only one thing, it can be unfair too."
- **Wed:** Play a simplified "fair sharing" game
- **Fri (Vibe Coding):** ScratchJr — make a "sharing" story

**Week 25 — "AI Doesn't Feel"**
- **Mon:** Role-play emotions — happy, sad, angry, excited. "Can a computer really feel these? Or just pretend?"
- **Wed:** If available, talk to a voice assistant and ask "Are you happy?" Discuss the answer
- **Fri (Vibe Coding):** ScratchJr — make characters show emotions through movement

**Week 26 — "When to Use AI, When to Use Your Brain"**
- **Mon:** Scenarios: "Should you use AI to...do your homework? help you spell a word? pick your best friend? find a recipe?"
- **Wed:** "Human or Computer?" sorting cards — which jobs need a human touch?
- **Fri (Vibe Coding):** ScratchJr — free build (most creative project yet!)

**Weeks 27–28 — "My AI Inventions"**
- Kids design their dream helpful robot over two weeks: what does it do, what can't it do, how would you teach it?
- Build a cardboard robot prototype
- **Fri (Vibe Coding):** ScratchJr — animate your dream robot

### Unit 6: Final Projects (Weeks 29–36)

- **Weeks 29–34:** Guided project work — kids choose one of:
  1. An "AI Storybook" (illustrated, with AI concepts woven in)
  2. A ScratchJr multi-screen interactive story
  3. A physical "Robot Museum" from crafts made all year
- **Week 35:** Rehearse presentations
- **Week 36:** 🎓 **End-of-Year AI Showcase** — present to family and friends

## 🎮 Vibe Coding Friday — Weekly Elective

### What Is Vibe Coding?

Vibe coding is describing what you want to build in plain language (or through visual blocks) and letting the tool bring it to life. For a 5-year-old, this means **ScratchJr** — dragging colorful blocks to make characters move, talk, and interact.

### Tools (All Free)

| Tool | What It Does | Link |
|---|---|---|
| **ScratchJr** (primary) | Drag-and-drop coding for ages 5–7. Make stories, games, animations | [scratchjr.org](https://www.scratchjr.org/) |
| **Code Karts** | Pre-reader coding through racing puzzles | App Store / Google Play |
| **Google Quick, Draw!** | Draw + watch AI guess (used in AI lessons too) | [quickdraw.withgoogle.com](https://quickdraw.withgoogle.com/) |
| **Google AutoDraw** | Doodle → AI-polished art | [autodraw.com](https://www.autodraw.com/) |
| **Chrome Music Lab** | Music + pattern exploration | [musiclab.chromeexperiments.com](https://musiclab.chromeexperiments.com/) |

### Vibe Coding Scope & Sequence (36 Weeks)

| Weeks | Phase | Skills | Projects |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1–4 | **Explore** | Open app, tap blocks, move characters | Make the cat walk, jump, spin |
| 5–9 | **Create** | Sequence blocks, add characters, add sounds | Simple 2-character animation |
| 10–14 | **Story** | Multi-page projects, voice recording, backgrounds | Interactive 3-page story |
| 15–18 | **Game Basics** | Tap reactions, simple cause-and-effect | "Tap the character" game |
| 19–23 | **Remix** | Copy and modify existing projects | Remix a friend's or template project |
| 24–28 | **Design** | Plan before building, sketch on paper first | "My Dream Game" blueprint → build |
| 29–34 | **Capstone** | Combine all skills into one project | Multi-page story OR game |
| 35–36 | **Showcase** | Present and explain what you built | Demo day! |

### Parent's Role During Vibe Coding

- Sit together — this is not solo screen time
- Let HIM drive the iPad/tablet
- Ask: "What do you want to happen next?" (not "here, let me do it")
- When something breaks: "That's great! What should we try differently?"
- Keep sessions to **20–30 minutes max**

## Quick-Reference: Conversation Starters

Use these during everyday moments — no lesson plan needed:

| Moment | What to Say |
|---|---|
| YouTube suggests a video | "How do you think it knew you'd like that?" |
| Phone autocompletes a word | "The phone is guessing! Is it right?" |
| Voice assistant answers | "Is Alexa alive? Does she feel happy?" |
| A photo app recognizes faces | "How did it know that's Grandma?" |
| A recommendation is wrong | "See? Even computers get things wrong!" |
| Playing with toys | "If a robot sorted your toys, what rule would you give it?" |

## Assessment (Observation-Based, Not Tests)

By year's end, look for these behaviors:

- [ ] Says "AI is not alive" or "computers don't have feelings" unprompted
- [ ] Notices AI in daily life ("that's the phone guessing!")
- [ ] Asks "Is that right?" when AI gives an answer
- [ ] Understands AI follows rules/patterns made by people
- [ ] Can explain what a ScratchJr project does
- [ ] Demonstrates safe habits (doesn't share personal info with devices)
- [ ] Shows confidence creating with technology as a tool
